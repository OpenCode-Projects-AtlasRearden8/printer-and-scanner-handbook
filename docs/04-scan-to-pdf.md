# Chapter 4: Scan to PDF

Scanning is where printers earn their keep. Two reliable routes: the scanner in your all-in-one, or the camera in your pocket. Use whichever is closer to the paper.

## Route 1: The all-in-one flatbed

Good for single sheets, odd sizes (insurance cards, ID cards), and anything fragile.

1. **Lift the lid, place the document face-down** on the glass, aligning it to the guide arrow near the top-left corner.
2. **From Windows:** Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Printers & scanners → your printer → Scan, or use the maker's utility. Choose **PDF** as the file type, not JPEG—PDFs keep pages together as one document.
3. **From Mac:** Open **Image Capture** or the Preview app (File → Import from Scanner…). Pick PDF as the format.
4. **From the printer's own touchscreen:** many all-in-ones can scan *straight to email or a network folder* with no computer involved. Set the destination once in the printer menu and future scans are a two-button operation.

Settings that matter when scanning:

- **Resolution (DPI):** 200 DPI is plenty for text you'll read on screen; 300 DPI if it will be printed or OCR'd. Higher just makes slow, heavy files.
- **Color:** scan in Color only for photos and signatures; Color/Grayscale for anything else.
- **Compression:** some utilities offer JPEG-in-PDF with a quality slider. Choose quality 60–80 for a good size/legibility trade-off.

## Route 2: Your phone — often the fastest scanner in your house

Your camera plus the right app beats most flatbeds for speed.

1. **Lay the document flat** on a dark, plain surface, with even light (avoid shadow bands from lamp arms).
2. **iPhone:** open the Files app → Browse → the ••• menu → *Scan Documents*, or the Notes app → camera icon → Scan Documents. It auto-detects page edges, corrects perspective, and makes a multi-page PDF in one go.
3. **Android:** open **Drive** → the `+` button → Scan, or the maker's printer app. Auto-capture on: hold steady; it takes the page when the edges align.
4. **Batch pages:** hold one button and keep turning pages of the stack; the app keeps appending them to one PDF.
5. **Name it meaningfully:** `2026-08-statement-acme.pdf` beats `scan10.pdf`. Future-you sends thanks.

**Legibility checklist before sending:**

- [ ] All four corners visible, nothing cut off
- [ ] Text dark and sharp at 100% zoom
- [ ] Pages in the right order
- [ ] File size sane (a 10-page scan under ~5 MB)

## Scanning multi-page stacks: the ADF

If your all-in-one has an automatic document feeder, put the stack (up to its page limit) in the feeder, choose *Scan → PDF*, and it pages through by itself. Feed odd or curled paper one-by-one on the glass; jams in the ADF path are the most common scanner jam (the flowchart covers the reverse too).

## Sign and send without a printer

Scanning is also how you turn "print, sign, photograph, email" into one step:

1. Scan or snap the document to a PDF.
2. In the scan app, use *Markup* (iOS) or *Annotate* (Android) to draw your signature or type it.
3. Save and email. Done in ninety seconds, zero paper.

> **Calm humor corner:** If your all-in-one's scan button works but its printer half doesn't, scan anyway. A scanner that doesn't print is still an extremely useful paper-to-PDF converter, and chapter 7 can usually revive the printer half.

## Which route, when?

| Situation | Best route |
| --- | --- |
| One page, fast, deadline | Phone |
| Multi-page stack (statements, forms) | ADF scan to PDF |
| Fragile, small, or thick items (ID cards, certificates) | Flatbed glass |
| Document I'll return signed | Phone scan + annotation |
| Document I'll reuse or OCR later | Flatbed at 300 DPI, color, PDF |
