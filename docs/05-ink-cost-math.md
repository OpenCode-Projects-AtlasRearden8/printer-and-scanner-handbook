# Chapter 5: Ink-cost math

The price sticker on a printer is the *cover charge*. The real cost of printing is what supplies cost over the life of the machine. This chapter is the friendly version of the arithmetic that printer ads quietly hope you skip.

## The two numbers you need

Every cartridge and toner bottle has two numbers printed somewhere unglamorous:

1. **Price** — what it costs to replace.
2. **Yield** — how many pages it's rated for (ISO standard, roughly a page of 5% coverage).

**Cost per page = Price ÷ Yield.** That's the whole formula. Here it is in action.

## Inkjet starter cartridges: the ambush

A "$59" inkjet arrives with starter cartridges rated for ~120 pages. Replacements are $35 each (often ~190 pages for black, less for color).

| | Starter | Replacement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | (in the box) | $35 |
| Yield | 120 pages | 190 pages |
| **Cost/page** | **(≈$0.29)** | **≈$0.18** |

Almost a *third of a dollar* per page in black-and-white. Printing 100 pages a month costs about $18/month—**$216 a year**—in a printer that cost $59. The printer is a payment plan.

## Refillable ink tank: the long game

A tank printer ($250–$400) ships with bottles rated for thousands of pages (often ~7,500 black / ~6,000 color).

| | Black bottle | One bottle lasts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | $12 ($30 set) | ~7,500 pages |
| **Cost/page** | **≈$0.002 black; ≈$0.01 color set** | years |

The same 100 pages/month now cost under **$1.50/month**. That's why tank printers pay for themselves in roughly a year at moderate printing, and why "buy the more expensive printer" is often the cheapest advice in this book.

## Monochrome laser: the unsexy hero

| | Toner (high-yield) |
| --- | --- |
| Price | $55 |
| Yield | 3,000 pages |
| **Cost/page** | **≈$0.018** |

And toner doesn't evaporate while you ignore it. For a household that prints 50–150 pages/month of text, the $180 laser all-in-one frequently has the lowest five-year cost of any option, subscription drama included.

## The five-year worksheet (copy this into your shopping notes)

For any printer you're considering, fill in these five blanks and compare:

```
Printer price:            $______
High-yield black:  price/yield: $______ / ______ pages
High-yield color (if any): price/yield: $______ / ______ pages
Pages per month (estimate): ______
Paper cost: ≈ $5 per 500 sheets
```

Then:

```
Yearly supplies ≈ (pages/month × 12 ÷ black yield × black price)
              + (pages/month × 12 ÷ color yield × color price × 0.3 avg color use)
              + (pages/month × 12 ÷ 100 × 1.0)   ← paper dollars
Five-year total ≈ printer price + 5 × yearly supplies
```

Rough, honest numbers beat perfect spreadsheet agony. The point isn't cent-level precision; it's catching a 10× difference.

## FAQs the numbers answer

**"Should I buy generic/compatible cartridges?"** For lasers, high-yield compatibles are usually fine and cheap. For inkjets, quality is unpredictable, and printer makers are litigious about it; if you go this way, read recent reviews for the *exact* cartridge number, not the printer.

**"Is an ink subscription worth it?"** It can be, if your usage is steady and inside a plan tier (families that print regularly love them). It's poor value if your printing is sporadic—the cheapest plan can still cost more than a tank printer's entire year. The test: is your *average* monthly count stable?

**"Does 'XL' matter?"** Yes, dramatically. XL cartridges cost 20–50% more and yield 100–300% more pages. Buying XL is the single highest-value move in inkjet shopping.

> **Calm humor corner:** The most expensive printer is the one that's cheap every time you press Print. The second most expensive is the one that's *also careful with your patience*.
