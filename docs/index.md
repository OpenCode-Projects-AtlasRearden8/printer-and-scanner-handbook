# The Printer & Scanner Handbook

*A manual for the machine everyone loathes, explained kindly.*

**By Asdale Dynamics, LLC** — part of Dylan Asdale's series of beginner-friendly guides that explain technology "like a friend would": plain English, step by step, never condescending.

---

Printers have a special talent. They work perfectly for months, then refuse to print a boarding pass fourteen minutes before you need to leave. This handbook exists to end that drama—or at least to make printer trouble boring instead of urgent.

Here's the promise, right up front:

- **You will learn why printers act this way.** (Spoiler: it's not because they hate you. Usually.)
- **You will learn how to buy a printer that stays out of your way.**
- **You will be able to fix nine out of ten printing problems yourself** in under fifteen minutes, without calling anyone.
- **You will learn the real math of ink and toner**, so the "cheap" printer never ambushes you again.
- **You will scan documents to PDF** using the phone you already own or the all-in-one you already have.

## The one-page version

If you only remember three things:

1. **Printers fail in predictable ways.** 90% of failures are: no power/no connection, a stuck print job, or empty/dried supplies. The [flowchart](troubleshooting-flowchart.html) covers all three.
2. **Buy for the cost of supplies, not the cost of the box.** Chapter 5 shows the math.
3. **The spooler is usually innocent-looking.** When a printer "does nothing," a jammed old job is often the culprit. Cancel all jobs, then try again. It works suspiciously often.

## A note about calm

Most printer problems cause panic because they happen *on a deadline*. A rule worth stealing from nursing: come back to the problem in the order of the flowchart, not in the order of your annoyance. Printers respond poorly to frustration but quite well to checklists.

Oh—and you can't break a printer by reading its manual or clicking through its menus. Good printing is mostly confident clicking.
