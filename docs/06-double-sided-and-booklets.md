# Chapter 6: Double-sided and booklets

Double-sided printing (duplex) is a feature you'll use forever. Booklet printing is a party trick that makes program sheets, zines, and recipe cards feel handmade. Both live in the same dialog.

## Printing double-sided, three ways

**Way 1: The printer does it (auto-duplex).** In the print dialog (Windows: *More settings*; Mac: appears in the main panel), check **Print on both sides** or **Two-sided**. If your printer supports it, trust it—flip-along-long-edge for normal reading, flip-along-short-edge for landscape pages that you scroll like a calendar.

**Way 2: Duplex without duplex hardware (manual).** Print odd pages only, flip the stack, print even pages only.

1. Print → **Odd pages only**.
2. Take the stack, let it cool a second, and place it back in the tray **printed-side facing correctly** for your printer (bottom feeders usually want printed side up, front-loader trays printed side down; experiment with 2 test pages first).
3. Print → **Even pages only**.

**Way 3: Phone photo mode.** When only one page matters, don't duplex. Duplexing a document you'll lose is a classic overnight miss.

## Page ranges: the mechanic behind everything

| You want | In the dialog |
| --- | --- |
| Pages 3–7 | `3-7` |
| Just page 2 and 9 | `2,9` |
| Odd pages only | check "odd pages" or `1-999/2` style—see your dialog |
| The last page only | use preview's current-page button |

## Booklet printing: the concept in one paragraph

A booklet takes your 8-page document and prints pages arranged so that, folded in half, they read in order: sheet 1 holds pages 8-and-1 on its outside and 2-and-7 on its inside, and so on. Software does the reordering; your only job is to fold once and staple along the fold (`saddle-stitch`, in printer-speak).

## Booklet printing by app

- **Microsoft Word:** Layout dialog → in the 2-pages-per-sheet style options, choose **Book fold**; Word handles matching margins automatically.
- **Adobe Acrobat Reader (free):** Print → **Booklet**. It does the reordering and page-imposition for any PDF.
- **LibreOffice Writer:** File → Print → set *Pages per sheet* to 2 with the "brochure" option, or use the Writer booklet layout preset.
- **Mac Preview:** the print dialog's layout popup has a booklet option in many apps; otherwise use Acrobat Reader.
- **The all-in-one's own menu:** many laser MFPs have a booklet button—place the *output* orientation test page first.

If your printer doesn't offer a booklet mode, "[imposition](glossary.html)" freeware and online tools can reorder any PDF into booklet order; then just print double-sided and fold.

## Folding tricks and staples

For a booklet of N pages, N should be a multiple of 4 (8, 12, 16). Extra blank pages at the end are normal—use them for notes. For the fold: use a fingernail or a ruler edge to crease sharply, and a long-reach stapler (or a saddle-stitch function on the MFP) for the spine. If your stapler is not a long-reach, fold first, then staple as deep as it reaches; a slightly inward staple is fine forever.

## When duplex jams (it's the flip mechanics, not you)

Auto-duplex sends the sheet through a second pass to print the back, then prints it. Jams here are really "second pass" jams:

- Don't force the duplex unit at the back—open the rear door fully and pull the page *straight* out along its path.
- Fanned paper (a quick riffle like shuffling cards) prevents sticking in humid rooms.
- Cheap recycled paper jams in duplex more than in simplex. If duplex jams weekly, the paper is guilty; switch brand before blaming hardware.
