# Glossary

The words printer manuals use, in the plain English of someone who remembers not knowing them. Alphabetical.

| Term | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **ADF** | *Automatic Document Feeder.* The slot where you stack several pages and the machine feeds them through the scanner by itself. |
| **All-in-one (AIO / MFP)** | Printer + scanner + copier (sometimes fax) in one box. The "MFP" in laser ads means the same thing: *multi-function printer*. |
| **Band (2.4 GHz / 5 GHz)** | The two lanes your Wi-Fi router broadcasts on. 2.4 goes farther; 5 is faster and shorter-range. Printers and phones must be on a lane they can both reach. |
| **Cartridge yield** | How many pages a cartridge or toner is *rated* to print (an ISO test number, roughly a page of 5% ink coverage). The other half of the cost-per-page formula. |
| **Cost per page** | Price ÷ yield. The only math that matters when comparing printers, as chapter 5 shows. |
| **DHCP reservation** | A router setting that promises a device (like your printer) the same IP address forever. The cure for printers that go "offline" every other week. |
| **Driver** | The small software translator between your computer and the printer. A bad driver causes gibberish or invisible jobs; reinstalling it fixes those, chapter 7-style. |
| **Duplex** | Printing on both sides of the paper. "Auto-duplex" = the printer flips pages itself; "manual duplex" = you flip, the printer prints odd pages then even. |
| **Duplex unit** | The little mechanical flap/door at the back that handles the flip in auto-duplex. Most "mystery jams" live here. |
| **Firmware** | The printer's built-in software. Occasionally needs updating; never power the printer off mid-update. |
| **Flatbed** | The glass scanning surface under the lid. Good for one page, books, and anything delicate. |
| **Head cleaning** | A utility cycle that squirts ink through clogged nozzles. Effective twice; wasteful endlessly. |
| **Imposition** | The reordering of pages so they land correctly when printed and folded as a booklet. Done by your software (Word's Book Fold, Acrobat's Booklet), not by you. |
| **ISO page yield** | The standardized test behind the yield number. Real-world mileage varies with how much ink your pages actually use. |
| **Monochrome** | Black-and-white only. Monochrome lasers are the unglamorous champions of home printing. |
| **Nozzle** | One of thousands of microscopic ink sprayers on an inkjet printhead. Clogged nozzles are why inkjets need regular use. |
| **Offline** | What the print dialog says when your computer can't reach the printer. Usually a network or queue issue, not a power issue (chapter 3). |
| **Print dialog** | The window that appears when you press Print; where paper size, scale, duplex, and page range live. |
| **Printhead** | The part that actually sprays the ink. Sometimes built into the cartridge, sometimes fixed in the printer. |
| **Queue / spooler** | The waiting line for print jobs, and the system service that manages it. Stuck first job = stuck whole line (chapter 7). |
| **Resolution (DPI)** | Dots per inch—how fine the print or scan is. 300 DPI for scans you'll print; 200 is fine for reading on screen. |
| **SSID** | Your Wi-Fi network's name, as shown in the list of networks. The printer must join the *same* SSID as the devices printing to it. |
| **Saddle-stitch** | The two staples along a booklet's folded spine. |
| **Toner** | Dry powder for laser printers, sealed in a cartridge. Doesn't evaporate; the reason lasers tolerate neglect. |
| **Waste toner box / Maintenance box** | The container that catches what the printing process throws away. When "printer full of ink can't print," this often needs emptying. |
| **WPS** | *Wi-Fi Protected Setup.* The push-buttons-to-pair shortcut between printer and router. |
| **XXL cartridge** | Marketing's highest-yield cartridge tier. Costs the most, wins on cost-per-page; usually the right buy if you'll use it all before it ages out. |
